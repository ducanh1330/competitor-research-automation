Most urgent finding

Smartlead has productised the agency model: full white-label at $29/month per client workspace, with tiers it explicitly describes as "built for agencies".

For an agency ICP this is the competitor to beat, not Apollo. White-label and per-client workspaces are the features an agency is actually shopping for, and Smartlead already ships both with published pricing.

Crowded language — claimed by more than one player

  • "All-in-one platform" — used verbatim by Apollo AND Lemlist. This is currently our stated differentiator and it is the most contested phrase in the category.
  • "AI agents" — Apollo, Instantly and Lemlist all ship named AI agents. Table stakes, not a claim.
  • "For everyone / at any scale" — Apollo and Lemlist both. Says nothing about who the product is for, which is precisely the gap an agency-specific product should exploit.

Unclaimed ground

  • Agency economics as the headline. Smartlead has agency features but still leads with inbox management; nobody leads with what an agency actually optimises — margin per client.
  • Cost that does not rise per client. Smartlead charges $29 per workspace; framing 'add clients for free, pay only for usage' is unclaimed and directly attacks the leader.
  • A named, published cost-per-qualified-lead. Every player prices inputs; agencies resell outcomes to their own clients and have to do this maths themselves.

Strengths & weaknesses vs DOZ.AI

Smartlead

Direct — agency-native

The agency competitor to beat. White-label, per-client workspaces and a unified multi-client inbox, with published pricing for all three.

Where they beat us

  • Full white-label at $29/mo per client workspace — we can verify no one else in the set offers this
  • Unified inbox across all campaigns and clients, led as their headline benefit
  • Unlimited email accounts on every plan, including the $39 Base
  • Explicit agency credibility — "trusted by 100,000+ businesses, from best agencies to Fortune 500"

Where we beat them

  • Their cost rises with every client won: $29 per workspace is a tax on agency growth
  • Real agency entry is $94 (Pro), not the $39 headline — workspaces are gated above Base
  • Tier-based, not usage-based, so an agency with uneven client volume overpays on quiet months
https://www.smartlead.ai/pricing2026-08-12

Where we beat them

Limitations

  • DOZ.AI is a demonstration company. Its tiers, credit unit and feature set are FICTIONAL, invented to exercise the pipeline end to end. Every competitor figure is real and sourced; nothing about DOZ.AI is.
  • Absence of an agency feature means it was not found on the pages captured — not proof it does not exist. Apollo and Clay may serve agencies through sales channels that leave no trace on public pages.
  • Apollo's /product URL redirects to its homepage, so that page adds nothing. The tracked page list should be corrected before the next run.
  • No traffic, keyword-volume or ad-spend data. Public page content only, no paid data vendors — so 'what's working' is reasoned from observable positioning, not measured performance.
  • Pages were captured logged-out from a UTC timezone. Regionally varied or account-specific pricing would not be visible.
  • Baseline run, so there is no month-over-month change section. The next run will produce one.
  • This analysis was revised twice. The first draft's lead finding — that per-seat pricing left us exposed — was withdrawn once credit-based pricing was confirmed. A second revision converted everything to AUD on an Australian-market assumption; that too was withdrawn when the market was confirmed as the US. Both are noted because a reader should know which conclusions were reversed and why.
  • All pages were captured via Firecrawl. Earlier snapshots in this run used local Playwright, which extracted materially less on JS-heavy pages (Apollo's homepage: 225 characters versus 9,182). All snapshots were re-taken with Firecrawl so the baseline is internally consistent — a mixed baseline would make next month's diff show extraction differences as if they were competitor changes.
